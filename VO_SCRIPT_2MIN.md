## SCRIPT

**[FACE] 1. PROBLEM**
> "Safe water is a basic need. But millions of people here have no quick way to know if the water they use every day is safe. Most WASH information sits in reports nobody reads. So people just guess."

**[FACE] 2. WHY ME**
> "I'm a Licensed Environmental Health Officer. This is the work I actually do. I built SanitovaCheck because people needed an answer now, not a report next quarter."

**[SCREEN] 3. WHAT IT IS**
> "SanitovaCheck is a free Android app. Point your camera at a water source or toilet facility, take a photo, add your location, and you get a risk assessment."

**[SCREEN] 4. THE DEMO**
> "This is the core. Take a photo, hit submit. Within seconds you get the observations, the risk level, and what to do next."

**[SCREEN] 5. THE HONEST PART**
> "I'll be straight with you. A photo can't confirm contamination. It can't tell you the water is safe to drink. What it does is flag what you can see — colour, leaks, what's nearby. So you know whether to act, and whether to call someone. It's a first check, not a lab result."

**[FACE] 6. HOW I BUILT + TESTED**
> "Built in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, with a Python backend and Qwen for the AI. Then I put it in front of real people. About thirteen testers first, more since. And some of those reports became actual fixes — the app now explains errors properly instead of showing you a technical message."

**[FACE] 7. PRO + CLOSING**
> "Scanning and learning are free — that's on purpose. Pro adds PDF reports and longer history for people who need proper records.
>
> I'm entering the RevenueCat Peace Prize, because getting this to the people who need it is the whole point. And the Kotlin award, because it's built entirely in Kotlin.
>
> It's live on Google Play. Leave an honest review — I'd rather have the truth than a nice number.
>
> SanitovaCheck — knowledge, safety, confidence."
